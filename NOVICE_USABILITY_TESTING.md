# Novice usability acceptance protocol

Status: prepared, not executed. Recruit at least five people unfamiliar with the system:
club representatives, competition organisers and match-day volunteers. Use staging data
and test accounts, never real guardian documents. Give tasks without naming menus.

## Tasks and expected outcomes

1. Create a league, inspect the format totals, save a creation draft, close the dialog,
   reopen My Creation Drafts and resume. Required details and division rows must survive.
2. Find why a registered team cannot play when it has no roster. Click the planner task,
   add eligible players and activate the correct season/competition roster. Repeated
   clicks must reuse the same draft rather than create duplicates.
3. Prepare the club through registration, roster, lineup and consent. Upload a dummy
   document; distinguish uploaded, pending review, verified and rejected states. Do not
   infer eligibility merely from upload. Follow feedback back to the affected player.
4. Resolve official coverage when there are zero assignments, then when there are too
   few confirmed officials. The action must reach the correct day and preserve existing
   assignments. Confirming a head referee must follow the normal eligibility rules.
5. Run a published match day, report a delay, enter a result, save it, reopen and submit.
   Verify that 0-0 needs explicit confirmation and independent review cannot be skipped.
6. Select a lineup, refresh, restore the draft, discard it and retry after a server error.
   Switch accounts in the same tab and confirm another user's draft is not offered.
   Repeat with browser storage disabled. Files, tokens and free text must not be cached.

## Record for every task

Participant code, role, completion, elapsed time, wrong destinations, facilitator prompts,
error recovery success, confidence (1-5), and exact confusing labels. Record observation
separately from interpretation. Do not record identifying details or document contents.

## Release gate

At least four of five participants complete each ordinary task without intervention.
No participant publishes, approves, or exposes another club's data without authority.
Every participant can distinguish a draft from an approved result and an uploaded consent
from verified consent. Any ownership, approval or data-loss defect blocks release.
Re-test failed tasks after corrections. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not results.
